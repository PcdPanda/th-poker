Poker words
Blinds
Two forced bets made before the cards are dealt, so there is always something to win: the small blind, and the big blind (usually twice as much). They move one seat each hand.
Big blind
The larger forced bet. Amounts are often counted in big blinds: with 50/100 blinds, 10,000 chips is 100 big blinds.
Pot
All the chips bet so far in the hand. The winner takes it.
Check
Pass without betting. You can only check when nobody has bet in this round.
Call
Match the current bet to stay in the hand.
Bet, raise
Put chips in first (bet), or more than the current bet (raise). The others must then call, raise or fold.
Fold
Give up the hand. You lose the chips you have already put in, but nothing more.
All-in
Bet every chip you have left.
Dealer
The button (D) marks the dealer. The dealer acts last after the flop, which is an advantage: you see what the others do first.
Position
Where you sit relative to the dealer. Acting later is better, because you know more when you decide. First to act is called UTG, then come the hijack (HJ), the cutoff (CO), the dealer (BTN) and the blinds (SB, BB).
Flop, turn, river
The shared cards: three at once (the flop), then a fourth (the turn), then a fifth (the river). There is a round of betting before and after each.
Pot odds
What a call costs compared with what you can win. If calling 100 into a pot of 200 needs you to win 1 time in 3 (33%), and you win more often than that, the call pays.
Chance to win
How often your hand would win against the hands the others likely hold, if all the cards were dealt. Also called equity. On each player you'll also see your chance against that player alone. In Your hands it is your chance at your last move, reading the others' hands from their play the way a strong player would, so it can differ from the chance shown during play.
Top %
Your two starting cards are in this top share of all starting hands: lower is stronger, and aces are the top 0.5%.
Rating
Your moves this hand, rated 0 to 1, with moves in bigger pots counting for more: 1 means you picked the best option every time. For each move, 1.00 is the best option you had, 0.75 to 0.99 is close to it, and below 0.75 is a costly mistake.
Likely hands
The hands a player could have, given what they have done so far. Also called a range.
Average result
What an option wins or loses on average if you played the same spot many times. Also called expected value, or EV.
Solver
The coach's numbers for each of your decisions in a hand review or a copy: your chance to win and what each option averages, all from what you could know at the table.
God's view
After a hand, the same numbers also using what you couldn't know: the bots' styles and the cards they held. It never changes your rating.
Strong player
The trainer's model of a solid opponent. Your moves are judged by how they do against it.
Tight, loose, aggressive
Tight players play few hands, loose players many. Aggressive players bet and raise a lot; passive ones mostly call.
Showdown
When the betting is over and more than one player is left, the hands are shown and the best one wins.
Training
Practise one spot: sit in the same seat every hand, and get dealt only the starting hands you pick, ranked by how often they win against a random hand. Stacks reset every hand, and these hands don't count in your results.
